# How State Corporate Tax Structures Shape Business Strategy and Growth

**M.S. Business Analytics thesis · Mercy University · 2024 · Caroline Stacia**

A 50-state analysis of corporate income tax rates from 2018 to 2023, compared against CNBC's
Top States for Business rankings, using regression and forecasting in R and Excel.

📄 [Read the thesis (PDF)](Caroline-Stacia_State-Corporate-Tax-Analysis_MS-Thesis-2024.pdf)

## Research questions
1. How have state corporate tax structures changed from 2018 to 2023?
2. How have those changes affected business decisions, especially geographic expansion?
3. What strategies can organizations use to optimize operations across different tax regimes?
4. What correlation exists between tax structure and in-state economic growth?

## Key findings
- **No corporate income tax:** Nevada, Ohio, Texas and Washington held a 0% rate every year studied.
- **Highest rates:** Minnesota (9.8%), Illinois (9.5%) and Pennsylvania (9.99% through 2022, 8.99% in 2023).
- **Taxes aren't everything:** a regression on CNBC's ten ranking categories explained 95.7% of the
  variation in 2021 state rank (adjusted R², n = 50, F = 110.2).
- **Forecast to 2025:** North Carolina's CNBC points rise to 1,708, Virginia's fall to 1,466,
  and New York holds steady near 1,317.
- **New York City:** ranked 27th of 51 on average corporate tax rate (6.5%) through 2021, then 32nd at 6.88%.

## Methods and tools
R / RStudio · Microsoft Excel · multiple linear regression · ANOVA · time-series forecasting

## Data sources
Tax Foundation state corporate income tax rates and brackets (2018–2023);
CNBC America's Top States for Business (2021–2023).
